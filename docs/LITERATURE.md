# Literature Review — Hard Problems Are Easy to Find

**Companion to [DESIGN.md](./DESIGN.md) and [PROBLEMS.md](./PROBLEMS.md). Status: v0.1, web-search-based survey (2026-06-11).**

Scope: prior work relevant to the thesis that complexity theory yields unbounded, contamination-proof supplies of problems that are hard for LLMs to solve by pure token generation, organized by the survey areas below. Claims are verified against paper abstracts/texts retrieved during the search unless flagged **[unverified]** (general knowledge, not independently confirmed in this pass).

---

## 1. Theory: transformer expressivity and complexity classes

The theoretical backbone of our Category 2 ("P but computationally deep") is now well established.

- **Transformers without CoT are (uniform) TC⁰.** Merrill, Sabharwal & Smith showed [saturated transformers are constant-depth threshold circuits](https://direct.mit.edu/tacl/article/doi/10.1162/tacl_a_00493/112604) (TACL 2022); Merrill & Sabharwal tightened this to log-precision transformers in L-uniform and then DLOGTIME-uniform TC⁰ (TACL 2023 / NeurIPS 2023) **[the specific venue/precision details from general knowledge; the TC⁰ chain itself verified]**. Chiang's [Transformers in Uniform TC⁰](https://arxiv.org/abs/2409.13629) (TMLR) extends this to poly(n)-bit precision — the bound is robust, not an artifact of precision assumptions. Consequence (assuming TC⁰ ≠ NC¹/NL/P): no fixed forward pass computes NC¹-complete problems (boolean formula evaluation, S₅ permutation composition), graph connectivity, or P-complete problems at scale.
- **CoT rents serial depth — at a precisely characterized price.** Merrill & Sabharwal, [The Expressive Power of Transformers with Chain of Thought](https://arxiv.org/abs/2310.07923) (ICLR 2024): logarithmically many decoding steps barely help; linear steps yield all regular languages (FSM simulation); polynomial steps yield exactly P — the first exact complexity characterization of a transformer variant. Li, Liu, Zhou & Ma, [CoT Empowers Transformers to Solve Inherently Serial Problems](https://arxiv.org/abs/2402.12875) (ICLR 2024): constant-precision transformers without CoT are only AC⁰, but T CoT steps simulate any size-T boolean circuit; empirically CoT helps most on inherently serial tasks (permutation composition, iterated squaring, circuit value). Feng et al., [Towards Revealing the Mystery behind Chain of Thought](https://arxiv.org/abs/2305.15408) (NeurIPS 2023 oral): bounded-depth transformers cannot directly answer basic arithmetic/equation tasks without super-polynomial size, yet constant-size autoregressive transformers solve them with CoT, and CoT covers a general class of dynamic-programming problems.
- **State tracking.** Merrill, Petty & Sabharwal, [The Illusion of State in State-Space Models](https://arxiv.org/abs/2404.08819) (ICML 2024): SSMs (Mamba, S4) are also TC⁰, so neither transformers nor SSMs can express NC¹-hard state tracking like S₅ word problems — directly motivating our Category 4 probes (object-swap tracking, permutation application).
- **Length generalization theory.** Zhou et al., [What Algorithms Can Transformers Learn?](https://arxiv.org/abs/2310.16028) (ICLR 2024) propose the RASP-L conjecture: transformers length-generalize on tasks expressible as short RASP-L programs (parity and standard-format addition are not). Huang et al. give a formal framework tying length-generalizable transformers to C-RASP (ICLR 2025, verified via citation only).

**Relation to our project:** this literature *predicts* our Category 2 cliffs (serial depth) and Category 4 failures (parity, S₅-style state tracking), and predicts that thinking budget should move the cliff roughly linearly in CoT tokens. We consume these theorems as hypotheses to test at difficulty-sweep resolution on frontier models; the theory papers themselves test small trained-from-scratch models, not production LLMs across calibrated difficulty knobs.

## 2. Empirical limits on algorithmic / compositional reasoning

- Dziri et al., [Faith and Fate: Limits of Transformers on Compositionality](https://arxiv.org/abs/2305.18654) (NeurIPS 2023): on multiplication, Einstein puzzles, and a DP task framed as computation graphs, models succeed by "linearized subgraph matching" (pattern reuse) and collapse with compositional depth (GPT-4: 59% on 3×3-digit multiplication, 4% on 4×4); errors compound multiplicatively — essentially our predicted accuracy ≈ (per-step)^depth signature, plus theoretical arguments for rapid decay.
- Mirzadeh et al., [GSM-Symbolic](https://arxiv.org/abs/2410.05229) (Apple, ICLR 2025): template-generated GSM8K variants show large variance under mere numerical-value changes, degradation with clause count, and up-to-65% drops from one irrelevant clause (GSM-NoOp) — evidence for distribution-matching rather than rule execution, and an early template-based instance-generation methodology.
- Arithmetic length generalization: [Abacus embeddings](https://arxiv.org/abs/2405.17399) (NeurIPS 2024) and [position coupling](https://arxiv.org/abs/2405.20671) push trained-from-scratch addition to ~6× training length, showing failures are partly representational (digit alignment) rather than purely computational — a caution for our presentation-format ablations.
- Lin et al., [ZebraLogic](https://arxiv.org/abs/2502.01100) (ICML 2025): programmatically generated logic-grid puzzles with quantified search-space size; accuracy collapses with search-space growth ("curse of complexity") and the collapse persists under model scaling and more inference compute. This is the closest existing "difficulty-parametrized cliff curve" work — for one problem family.

**Relation:** we generalize this style of measurement across a *taxonomy* of hardness sources with per-family classical baselines, rather than one task family per paper.

## 3. Planning and search benchmarks

- Valmeekam et al., [PlanBench](https://openreview.net/forum?id=YXogl4uQUO) (NeurIPS 2023) and the companion "[LLMs Still Can't Plan](https://openreview.net/pdf?id=wUU-7XTL5XO)" line: classic IPC domains (Blocksworld, Logistics); LLMs perform poorly, and *obfuscated* ("Mystery") Blocksworld — semantically identical, lexically scrambled — is catastrophic, exposing retrieval rather than planning. Their [o1 evaluation](https://arxiv.org/abs/2409.13373) (2024): o1 jumps to 97.8% on standard Blocksworld and makes first progress on Mystery variants, but degrades with plan length and remains far from solver reliability/cost. Kambhampati et al.'s position paper [LLMs Can't Plan, But Can Help in LLM-Modulo Frameworks](https://arxiv.org/abs/2402.01817) (ICML 2024) anchors the skeptical pole of the debate and proposes generate-test loops with sound external verifiers.
- Apple's [The Illusion of Thinking](https://machinelearning.apple.com/research/illusion-of-thinking) (June 2025): reasoning models on Tower of Hanoi / River Crossing / Blocks World / Checkers with parametric complexity show progressive decline then *complete collapse*, with thinking-token expenditure counterintuitively *decreasing* near the collapse point; providing the algorithm in-prompt didn't help. The rebuttal, Lawsen's [The Illusion of the Illusion of Thinking](https://arxiv.org/abs/2506.09250), argues much of the collapse is a token-budget artifact (Hanoi needs exponentially long outputs; models explicitly note truncation), that some River Crossing instances were unsolvable yet scored as failures, and that compact programmatic answer formats dissolve much of the collapse. (Reporting around this rebuttal was noisy — at least one circulated "rebuttal" was satirical; the arXiv comment above is the substantive one.) Net community takeaway: real degradation exists but headline "collapse" claims require careful output-format and budget controls — directly informing our design rules 4 (format-fault isolation) and the solution-length-vs-instance-hardness distinction (Hanoi is algorithmically trivial with exponential output; our generators should avoid conflating answer length with computational difficulty).
- Trained-searcher line: Lehnert et al., [Searchformer](https://arxiv.org/abs/2402.14083) — transformers trained on A* search traces solve Sokoban/mazes near-optimally, evidence that token-serialized search is *learnable* in-domain, sharpening the question of why general-purpose LLMs don't do it zero-shot.

## 4. NP-hard / complexity-flavored benchmarks

This is the most crowded area and the one our framing must position against most carefully.

- [NPHardEval](https://arxiv.org/abs/2312.14890) (ACL 2024): 900 questions across P / NP-complete / NP-hard with 10 difficulty levels, automated generation+verification, monthly refresh against contamination. Closest in spirit to our complexity-class organization, but: single difficulty scale, no classical-solver baselines as a reported contrast, no graceful-degradation (approximation-ratio) scoring, and pre-reasoning-model.
- [GraphArena](https://arxiv.org/abs/2407.00379) (ICLR 2025): 4 polynomial + 6 NP-complete graph tasks on real-world graphs; outputs classified correct / suboptimal / hallucinatory (well-formatted but infeasible) / missing — a scoring taxonomy we should adopt or cite for certificate-validity tracking. Finds persistent hallucination on larger instances even with CoT, code, and test-time-compute mitigations.
- **SAT at the phase transition (our problem 1.2 exists in the literature).** Hazra et al., "[Can LLMs Reason?](https://arxiv.org/abs/2408.07215)" and its successor "[Have LLMs Learned to Reason? A Characterization via 3-SAT Phase Transition](https://arxiv.org/abs/2504.03930)" (2025): LLMs show an inverted easy-hard-easy accuracy pattern around α ≈ 4.267; accuracy drops to ~10% in the hard region; DeepSeek-R1 is the outlier, with traces showing backtracking-like search. A 2026 follow-up ([Satisfiability Solving with LLMs](https://arxiv.org/html/2605.28602v1)) finds conventional metrics misleading (models classify nearly everything SAT) and sharp degradation with variable count.
- TSP/combinatorial-optimization evals: GPT-class models give ~133% optimality gaps on 50-node TSP and ~50% feasibility on <30-node CO problems (surveyed in [LLMs as End-to-end CO Solvers](https://arxiv.org/abs/2509.16865)); CO-Bench (AAAI 2026) benchmarks reasoning models against classical solvers under time budgets. Microsoft's [Inference-Time Scaling for Complex Tasks](https://arxiv.org/abs/2504.00294) (2025) is particularly close to us: synthetically generated TSP and 3-SAT with controlled difficulty levels and exact ground truth, analyzing both accuracy *and token usage* versus difficulty across o1/R1-class models — i.e., a partial version of our thinking-budget-vs-difficulty axis, for two families.

## 5. Procedurally generated / contamination-resistant evals

- [DyVal](https://arxiv.org/abs/2309.17167) (ICLR 2024 spotlight): DAG-based dynamic generation of reasoning tasks with controllable complexity; models that shine on static benchmarks drop sharply on freshly generated instances — the canonical "dynamic evaluation protocol" citation. NPHardEval's monthly refresh and GSM-Symbolic's templates are the other established mechanisms. LiveBench-style continually-refreshed benchmarks and task-contamination studies also exist **[unverified]**. The *methodological* idea of generator-not-dataset is therefore established; what is rarer is generators with (a) parametric difficulty calibrated per model, (b) shipped reference solvers, and (c) certified optima/bounds for gradated scoring.

## 6. Chaotic systems and dynamical prediction

- Liu et al., [LLMs learn governing principles of dynamical systems](https://arxiv.org/abs/2402.00795): LLaMA-2 extrapolates trajectories (including the logistic map at r=3.9) zero-shot, with accuracy scaling in context length.
- Zhang & Gilpin, [Zero-shot forecasting of chaotic systems](https://arxiv.org/abs/2409.15771) (ICLR 2025): 135 chaotic systems × 10⁸ points; time-series foundation models are competitive short-horizon and preserve attractor geometry after point forecasts fail. The follow-up [Context Parroting](https://arxiv.org/abs/2505.11349) shows much of this success is motif-copying (≈1-NN in delay-embedding space) and ties context-scaling exponents to the attractor's fractal dimension.
- These works are *numeric-sequence* forecasting (given trajectory context). We found no work that gives the *symbolic map definition + exact initial condition* in-prompt and scores exact digit-level iteration against Lyapunov-predicted horizons under a thinking-budget sweep — our 3.1 framing. Closest adjacent: [LifeGPT](https://arxiv.org/abs/2409.12182) (trained-from-scratch Game-of-Life simulation; error accumulates under recursion) and 1D-CA reasoning benchmarks (e.g., an [OpenReview 1dCA benchmark](https://openreview.net/pdf?id=dZVpyo3ZGz) separating rule induction from state propagation), which support our 2.2 automaton family but use trained models or rule-induction framings.

## 7. Calibration: knowing when you can't

- [AbstentionBench](https://arxiv.org/abs/2506.09038) (NeurIPS 2025): 20 datasets of unanswerable/underspecified questions; abstention is unsolved, and *reasoning fine-tuning degrades it* (−24% on average) even in math domains.
- [Answering the Unanswerable Is to Err Knowingly](https://arxiv.org/abs/2508.18760) (2025): reasoning models often internally detect that a problem is flawed yet answer anyway — a cognition/behavior misalignment.
- This literature targets *ill-posed* questions. Whether models recognize well-posed but *computationally infeasible-for-them* instances (e.g., "this 40-step Hénon iteration exceeds what I can do digit-exactly") — graceful degradation versus confident confabulation as a function of a difficulty knob — appears essentially unstudied. The Illusion-of-Thinking observation that thinking tokens *shrink* past the collapse point is the only adjacent datapoint.

## 8. Gaps and positioning

**Candid crowdedness assessment.** Each *individual* claim of ours has neighbors, several of them strong:

- "LLMs fail on NP-hard combinatorics, measured by complexity class" — crowded (NPHardEval, GraphArena, SAT-phase-transition papers, CO-Bench).
- "Accuracy cliffs versus a difficulty knob on procedurally generated instances" — established for single families (ZebraLogic for CSPs; Illusion of Thinking for puzzles; Inference-Time Scaling for TSP/3-SAT).
- "Contamination-proof generator methodology" — established in principle (DyVal, NPHardEval, GSM-Symbolic).
- "Theory says transformers+CoT have bounded serial power" — mature and rigorous (Merrill–Sabharwal; Li et al.; Feng et al.).
- "Models pattern-match rather than execute algorithms" — repeatedly shown (Faith and Fate, GSM-Symbolic, Mystery Blocksworld).

**Where genuine novelty plausibly lies** (none of the following was found in this search):

1. **A unified complexity-theoretic taxonomy spanning all four hardness sources in one suite with one methodology.** Existing benchmarks each occupy one cell (NP-hard *or* CSP *or* planning *or* arithmetic). No work systematically contrasts "hard for everyone" vs. "P-but-deep" vs. "inverse/search" vs. "mechanism probes" on the same models with the same scoring discipline. The taxonomy *as the contribution* is open.
2. **Forward/inverse controlled pairs** (iterate a map ↔ recover the preimage; evaluate f ↔ argmax f) holding surface form fixed while flipping complexity class. We found nothing doing this within-family comparison.
3. **Lyapunov-predicted horizons.** Theory-derived quantitative predictions of the achievable horizon (digits-correct vs. k given λ) tested against LLM behavior — no prior work found; chaos-forecasting work is numeric/context-based, not symbolic-definition-based.
4. **Thinking-budget vs. difficulty scaling as a primary measured axis across hardness categories.** Partial precedents exist (Inference-Time Scaling does tokens-vs-difficulty for TSP/3-SAT; Illusion of Thinking reports the token-collapse anomaly), but a systematic off/default/max sweep across a taxonomy, asking "does budget move the cliff or merely delay it, and does the answer depend on *why* the problem is hard?", is open.
5. **Classical-baseline contrast as a headline metric** ("10 lines of Python / microseconds vs. frontier model at max thinking budget"), including *does the model beat greedy/nearest-neighbor* — GraphArena reports suboptimality but no one foregrounds the solver-cost asymmetry as the central artifact.
6. **Computational-infeasibility calibration** (knowing it's beaten on well-posed instances), distinct from the ill-posedness abstention literature.

**Risks confirmed by the survey.** (a) The "unfairness" critique is real and pre-litigated — the Illusion-of-Thinking episode shows reviewers will attack output-format/token-budget confounds first, so our format-fault isolation, answer-length-vs-hardness decoupling, and unsolvability handling must be airtight. (b) DeepSeek-R1-class models already show qualitative search behavior on SAT; claims must be stated as measured cliffs, not categorical impossibility. (c) For SAT and TSP specifically we are incremental; those families earn their place as calibration anchors connecting to known literature, while the novel families (chaos horizons, forward/inverse pairs, automaton depth knobs) carry the novelty claim.
