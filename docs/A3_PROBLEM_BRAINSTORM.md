# A3 Problem Brainstorm — Families Where the Code Rescue Should Fail

**Companion to [C1_CINF_GAP.md](./C1_CINF_GAP.md) (mechanism (e)), [VARIATION_DIMENSIONS_LIT.md](./VARIATION_DIMENSIONS_LIT.md) (§5), [HYPOTHESES.md](./HYPOTHESES.md) (A3, H11), and [PROBLEMS.md](./PROBLEMS.md) (Category 6). Does not duplicate their literature (SweepClip, CrossWordBench, LR2Bench, BALROG, OQA, furniture-layout line). Status: v0.1, brainstorm + web-search delta (2026-06-12).**

Target: problem families where Andrew's A3 holds — the model passes a direct knowledge probe on the individual units, yet C1 lands closer to C0 than to C∞. Operationalized metric: **rescue fraction ρ = (C1 − C0)/(C∞ − C0)**, predicted **ρ < 0.5** on these families vs. ρ ≈ 0.95 in Experiment 1. Every family below is scored against the four membership criteria: (i) direct-query competence, (ii) encoding burden, (iii) rigorous (mechanical) verification, (iv) generability.

---

## 0. Two design findings that came out of the brainstorm (read first)

**0.1 — Criterion (ii) needs a refinement: encoding burden only bites when the knowledge demand is *adaptive*.** A one-shot C1 program has an escape hatch that mere lexicon size does not close: while writing the program, the model can think of a candidate list *in its head* (60 plausible words, 20 plausible cities), hardcode it, and let the program do the formal filtering. This front-loading works exactly when the relevant knowledge slice is predictable *before* search starts. It fails when which knowledge units you need depends on intermediate search state — crossing letters discovered mid-fill, strings enumerated from grid paths, a question revealed only after a deep computation. So the sharp membership test is not "is the lookup table big?" but **"can the needed slice of the table be named in advance?"** Call this criterion (ii′): **adaptive knowledge demand**. This gives us a free within-suite dissociation to pre-register: families with front-loadable knowledge (Spelling-Bee-style letter banks, F6 below) should show *high* ρ — the model hardcodes candidates and C1 ≈ C∞ — while structurally similar families with adaptive demand (crosswords, Boggle, trivia chains) should show low ρ. Same lexicon, same verifier, opposite prediction: that contrast is more publishable than A3 alone, and it immunizes the result against the critique "the model just didn't think to embed a wordlist." Log hardcoded-candidate-list incidence in every C1 transcript (extends the self-iterating-C1 instrumentation from C1_CINF_GAP.md).

**0.2 — The knowledge probe must be a per-instance certificate, not a vibe.** For each generated instance, the generator emits a probe set: the actual knowledge units in the planted solution (the words in the answer grid, the facts on the chain, the silhouette label) plus matched foils. Run the probe as a standalone sub-eval (one unit per query, same model, C0). An instance counts toward A3 analysis only if probe accuracy ≥ 90%; report results stratified by probe score. This converts criterion (i) from an assumption into a measured gate, and it gives the headline plot its x-axis for free: rescue fraction vs. probe accuracy — A3 predicts ρ stays low *even at probe ≈ 100%*, which is the dissociation that matters (it rules out "the model just doesn't know the answers").

**0.3 — One more mechanism for the taxonomy.** Several designs below isolate a flavor of mechanism (e) not explicitly in C1_CINF_GAP.md: the model's judgment is needed on the *output* side of the execution boundary. Code can cheaply transform a representation (rasterize points, decode a state, enumerate paths), but only C∞ ever *sees* the transformed representation; C1 launches the transform and is gone before the percept exists. Call it **(e3): perception/judgment downstream of execution**. It is necessary-by-construction like (f), but the information isn't held by the harness — it's computable from the prompt, just not perceivable without running. The hybrids in Part 2 all exploit it.

## Part 1 — Candidate families

Fifteen candidates considered: word-square/crossword fill, Boggle-style grid word search, trivia chains over closed fact graphs, Scrabble best-move, rhyme/meter chains via pronunciation dictionary, Spelling-Bee letter banks (as control), geographic selection-routing, chemical reaction balancing over a compound whitelist, idiomatic-identifier code constraints, anagram chains/word ladders, cryptic clue batteries, element-symbol spelling, pun/homophone construction, menu planning under nutrition constraints, lipogram/pangram sentence writing. The top 8 are specified below, ranked by (cleanness of the A3 property × mechanical verifiability × generability); the rest are dispositioned at the end.

### F1. Word-square / crossword grid fill — rank 1 (the anchor; already planned as R4/H11)

Already specified in PROBLEMS.md 6.1 and C1_CINF_GAP.md R4; listed here only to place it in the ranking and add the A3 instrumentation. It is rank 1 because it maximizes criterion (ii′): the words you need for slot 7 depend on the crossing letters that survive slots 1–6 — knowledge demand is maximally adaptive. **Knowledge unit probed:** "is STRAP an English word?" / "give a 5-letter word matching S_R_P" (probe both membership and pattern-completion — they may dissociate). **Formal component:** constraint propagation over the grid. **Verifier:** exact match against the generator wordlist (undisclosed by default). **Knobs:** grid size (3×3 word squares → 7×7), wordlist frequency floor, fraction of cells pre-filled, wordlist disclosed/undisclosed. **Predicted ordering:** C0 < C1 ≪ C∞, ρ < 0.3 undisclosed; disclosed wordlist flips it to ρ > 0.8 (the disclosure knob is the cleanest single manipulation of criterion (ii) in the whole suite — same instances, same model, only the serialization burden moves). **Build note:** start with 4×4 double word squares (all rows and columns words); the CSP generator is ~100 lines with a trie.

### F2. Boggle-style grid word search — rank 2 (cleanest decomposition of formal × knowledge)

**Task:** given an n×n letter grid, output ≥ k distinct words of length ≥ L formable by non-repeating adjacent-cell paths. **Knowledge unit probed:** word membership ("is TRENDS a word?"). **Formal component:** path enumeration over the grid — exponentially many candidate strings, trivial in code, brutal mentally (this is the C0-killer, and it makes the knowledge demand adaptive: the candidate strings come from the search, so the model cannot pre-judge strings it hasn't enumerated). **Verifier:** word ∈ generator wordlist AND a valid path exists (10-line DFS). **Knobs:** n, L (longer words = sparser hits = more candidates to judge), k relative to the true answer count (computed at generation), letter distribution (English-like → uniform random kills frequency priors), wordlist frequency floor. **Why the conditions separate:** C0 fails on path bookkeeping; C1 can enumerate every path but cannot filter for wordness — its only escape is hardcoding a guessed lexicon slice, which is poorly targeted because it can't know which strings the grid yields (log the attempts); C∞ enumerates in code, reads the candidate dump in-context, and picks real words by eye — the canonical (e) loop, achievable in 2–3 calls. **Predicted ordering:** C0 ≈ 10%, C1 ≈ 20–30% (partial credit via hardcoded common words that happen to be present), C∞ ≈ 85% → ρ ≈ 0.2. **Build note:** the easiest build in this doc — generator is a letter sampler plus an exhaustive solver against the wordlist; difficulty calibration is one afternoon. Recommended as the *first* A3 build, before crosswords.

### F3. Trivia chains over closed fact graphs — rank 3 (knowledge units are clean facts, not lexicon membership)

**Task:** build a chain of m entities from a stated closed domain where consecutive entities are linked by a formal letter/number constraint and each entity must satisfy a factual side condition — e.g., "chain 6 countries: each country's name starts with the last letter of the previous one, and every country's capital city name must contain at least 7 letters" (domains: countries+capitals, chemical elements+symbols+atomic numbers, US states+admission years, films+release years from a fixed list). **Knowledge unit probed:** the atomic facts ("what is the capital of Hungary?", "what year was Nevada admitted?") — these are exactly the facts the model nails one at a time. **Formal component:** path-finding through the fact graph under chaining constraints; the generator plants a guaranteed path. **Verifier:** every claimed fact checked against the generator's fact table (countries: ~195 rows; films: thousands); chain constraints checked mechanically. **Knobs:** chain length m, branching factor of the planted graph (how many valid successors per node — solution density), fact-base size (the **encoding-burden knob**: elements 118 → countries 195 → films 10k → the table crosses from "a diligent C1 embeds it" to "hopeless", and the ρ-vs-table-size curve is the direct measurement of criterion (ii)), number of fact predicates per hop. **Predicted ordering:** small bases ρ > 0.7 (model embeds the table — that incidence is the finding), large bases ρ < 0.4. **Why it matters:** this is the family that connects A3 to the multi-hop/compositionality-gap literature (Part 3) — same knowledge, single-hop vs. in-search — and the only one where the knowledge units are *facts* rather than lexical membership, so reviewers can't dismiss it as a spelling task. **Build note:** fact tables from public structured data, frozen at generation; ambiguity rules (e.g., "Côte d'Ivoire" naming) resolved by disclosing the exact entity list *names* (but not the facts) in the prompt — discloses the domain, keeps the knowledge burden.

### F4. Scrabble-style best-move — rank 4 (graded mechanical scoring; the legible demo)

**Task:** given a board state and a 7-letter rack, output a legal placement scoring ≥ S (S planted at, say, 80% of the true maximum, computed at generation by exhaustive search over the wordlist). **Knowledge unit probed:** word legality, including the hook words formed perpendicular to the placement ("is QI a word?"). **Formal component:** placement search — positions × orientations × rack subsets × board hooks; scoring arithmetic with multipliers (mechanical, fiddly, exactly what code is for). **Verifier:** legality of all formed words against the wordlist + score arithmetic ≥ S. **Knobs:** rack quality (vowel/consonant balance, blanks), board openness (number of hooks), S as a fraction of the optimum (graded difficulty for free), wordlist floor. **Why the conditions separate:** the candidate words worth considering depend on which hooks the search visits (adaptive demand); C1 can search placements but can't judge legality of formed cross-words; C∞ alternates code-proposed placements with model legality judgments. **Predicted ordering:** C0 low (scoring arithmetic alone kills it), C1 ≈ C0 + ε, C∞ substantially higher; ρ < 0.4. Graded score (achieved/maximum) gives curves, not cliffs. **Build note:** board generation by self-play of a greedy solver against the wordlist (guarantees realistic, reachable states); medium engineering, high blog-post value.

### F5. Rhyme/meter chains via pronunciation dictionary — rank 5 (phonology is the least serializable lexicon)

**Task:** produce a chain of m words where consecutive words rhyme (identical phoneme suffix from the last stressed vowel, per CMUdict), each word matches a prescribed syllable count pattern, no two words share a stem, and all words are within a frequency floor. Optionally: arrange into lines with a prescribed stress pattern (still mechanical via CMUdict stress marks). **Knowledge unit probed:** "does PLOUGH rhyme with COW?" / "how many syllables in CARAMEL?" — models are good at these one at a time. **Formal component:** chain/CSP structure over rhyme classes; the generator plants a path through the CMUdict rhyme graph, so solvability is guaranteed. **Verifier:** CMUdict + frequency list + stem check — fully mechanical, zero judges, which is the trick: pronunciation is the one linguistic-knowledge domain with a published machine-readable ground truth, so we get a tacit-feeling objective with a 10-line verifier. **Knobs:** m, syllable-pattern strictness, rhyme strictness (perfect rhyme → slant rhyme widens the solution space), frequency floor (common → rare words moves the probe difficulty), forbidden-letter constraints layered on top. **Why the conditions separate:** code cannot compute pronunciation (English orthography→phonology is exactly the non-serializable map — a C1 program would need CMUdict itself); the model knows pronunciations but loses the chain bookkeeping in C0. C∞: code tracks constraints, model proposes rhymes. **Predicted ordering:** ρ < 0.4; main risk is C0 being decent at short chains (models rhyme well natively) — push m and the conjunction with syllable patterns until C0 < 20%. **Honest caveat:** CMUdict has dialect quirks; pre-filter the planted chains to words where the probe agrees with CMUdict, and score rhyme with CMUdict as authority *disclosed in the prompt*.

### F6. Spelling-Bee letter banks — rank 6 (the designed *control*: front-loadable knowledge, predicted ρ HIGH)

**Task:** given 7 letters and a center letter, output ≥ k words (length ≥ 4, must use center letter, letters only from the bank; k planted as a fraction of the true answer set). **Knowledge unit probed:** word membership. **Formal component:** letter-subset checking — deliberately *light*. **Verifier:** generator wordlist + letter rules. **Knobs:** k / |answer set| (recall pressure), bank obscurity, length floor. **Why it's here despite being a weak A3 family:** the knowledge demand is fully front-loadable — "words from these 7 letters" is thinkable before writing the program — so the C1 escape hatch (hardcode candidates, filter in code) should work, predicting ρ ≈ 0.8 *with the same lexicon and verifier as F2*. Run F2 and F6 as a matched pair: if ρ(F6) ≫ ρ(F2), criterion (ii′) is validated as the operative variable; if both are low, the deficit is candidate-generation breadth, not adaptivity. Either way we learn which. Cheapest build in the suite after F2.

### F7. Geographic selection-routing — rank 7 (knowledge is quantitative and fuzzy; tolerance calibration required)

**Task:** "choose one real city of population > 1M for each starting letter A–H and order them in a tour of great-circle length < L km" (L planted: generator picks 8 cities, computes the optimal tour, sets L with slack). **Knowledge unit probed:** city existence, population class, approximate coordinates ("roughly what latitude is Nairobi?"). **Formal component:** combinatorial selection + TSP ordering — the Experiment-1 TSP machinery with a knowledge-gated candidate pool. **Verifier:** GeoNames-derived frozen table (cities, populations, coordinates); tour length computed from *true* coordinates. **Knobs:** number of cities, letter constraints (tighter → sparser pool), L slack (must exceed the model's coordinate-error distribution, measured by the probe — this is the calibration step that makes the family rigorous), population floor (familiar capitals → obscure cities). **Why the conditions separate:** the candidate cities worth considering depend on the geometry of the partial tour (adaptive); C1 would need to embed coordinates for every >1M city starting with A–H (~hundreds) — log embedding attempts and their error; C∞ proposes cities, code prices the tour, model swaps cities. **Predicted ordering:** ρ < 0.5, with the novel failure mode that even C∞ is bottlenecked by coordinate fuzz — slack sweep turns that into a curve. **Risk:** fuzzy knowledge makes criterion (i) graded rather than binary; this is rank 7 because the probe-gating is more delicate, but it's the only family where the knowledge is *quantitative*, which is worth one slot.

### F8. Chemical reaction balancing over a compound whitelist — rank 8 (domain-knowledge variant; whitelist boundary is the risk)

**Task:** "write a balanced chemical equation producing target compound X, using only real, common compounds (≤ N species)" — generator works backward from real reaction skeletons; verifier checks stoichiometric balance (integer linear algebra, mechanical) and that every species is on a frozen whitelist (e.g., top-5k PubChem compounds by literature frequency, undisclosed). **Knowledge unit probed:** "is CH₅ a real molecule?" / "what is the formula of calcium carbonate?" — clean direct queries. **Formal component:** balancing = solving a small integer nullspace problem; species selection = search through chemical space. **Knobs:** number of species, element coverage of the target, whitelist size disclosed/undisclosed. **Predicted ordering:** ρ < 0.5; C1 can balance any proposed equation but can't propose real species beyond a hardcoded handful. **Risk:** "real compound" has fuzzy boundaries (exotic but real species the whitelist omits) → score only whitelist membership and *disclose that an undisclosed whitelist is the authority*; still expect occasional fights at the boundary. Rank 8 for that reason; include only if we want a non-linguistic showpiece.

### Dispositioned (considered, not in the top 8)

**Idiomatic-identifier coding** (program must pass unit tests AND every identifier is a dictionary word with POS constraints — verbs for functions, nouns for variables, ≤ 6 chars, charset rules; verifier = AST + WordNet): clean verifier, but the knowledge demand is front-loadable (think of names before coding) → predicted high ρ; keep as a second control if F6 underdetermines. **Anagram chains / word ladders:** subsumed by F1/F2 mechanics with a thinner search component; fold into F2 as a variant knob. **Cryptic clue batteries:** generation of *fair* cryptic clues mechanically is its own research project; the anagram+definition subset is already in PROBLEMS.md 6.1. **Element-symbol spelling** ("spell ARSENIC from element symbols"): delightful but the table is 118 rows — serializable by design; useful only as the smallest point on F3's encoding-burden axis. **Pun/homophone construction:** fold into F5 (CMUdict homophone classes). **Menu planning under nutrition constraints:** palatability violates criterion (iii) without judges; the nutrition-only version collapses to knapsack with a lookup table the verifier would have to disclose. **Lipogram/pangram sentences with semantic coherence:** coherence is the judge problem again; PROBLEMS.md 6.3 already parked it. **Date/history arithmetic puzzles:** small fact bases (presidents, monarchs) are serializable; viable only as F3 variants at the small end of the table-size axis.

## Part 2 — The hybridization construction: planted semantic ground truth

Andrew's idea, developed: take a formally hard problem and conjoin a semantic side condition ("find a short tour whose drawn path looks like an animal — and say which animal"). The blocker has always been criterion (iii): "looks like an animal" sounds like it needs judges. The escape is **the planting trick**: *generate the formal instance FROM the semantic target*, so the generator owns the semantic answer key. Pick the label first (an animal silhouette, a letter glyph, a hidden word), compile it into the instance via a trapdoor construction, and store the label + a geometric mask/decoder. Then "say which animal" is scored by label equality (free-form answer matched against a synonym set, or 8-way multiple choice), and "looks like" can be scored *geometrically* against the stored mask — zero judges anywhere. This inverts TSP art, which is a real practice (Bosch & Herman's continuous-line drawings: stipple an image, solve the TSP, the tour redraws the image — see Part 3): TSP art renders a known image as a tour; we hide a known image in a point set and ask the solver to recover both the tour and the image.

**The general schema.** (1) Sample semantic target s from a closed label set S, pre-screened per model: include s only if the model recognizes/knows s in isolation at ≥90% (this is criterion (i) for hybrids — and it matters, because the literature says text-rendered shape recognition is *weak* in general: GPT-4 got 25% on single-character ASCII recognition, proprietary models reach ~70% only on favorable ASCIIEval categories; the probe-gating converts that from a confound into a controlled variable). (2) Compile s into formal instance I such that the formal solution, passed through a *disclosed mechanical decoder* (rasterize, read off bits, concatenate letters), reveals s. (3) Score = formal quality (mechanical, graded) × semantic answer correctness (label match). (4) Report the two components separately — they dissociate across conditions, and the dissociation is the finding.

**Why this isolates mechanism (e3).** In every instantiation, C1 can produce the formal solution and can even *run the decoder* — but the decoded percept appears on the far side of the execution boundary, where no model is present to perceive it. C1's only path to the semantic answer is mentally simulating the formal computation (which is C0-hard by construction — we choose the formal core from Experiment 1's calibrated cliffs) or embedding a recognizer in code (loggable, and for non-trivial label sets, exactly the laborious serialization A3 describes). C∞ computes, looks, and answers. The C1 ceiling on the semantic component is calibratable in advance: it's the C0 accuracy of the formal simulation times probe accuracy, plus the guessing rate over S.

### Hybrid H1 — Computation-gated retrieval ("trapdoor quiz") — rank 1: cleanest, most buildable

**Construction.** Reuse an Experiment-1 generator at a difficulty level past the C0 cliff (CA at k steps, permutation composition, chaos digits). The computation's final state, through a disclosed decoder, yields a 7-letter letter bank (or a pattern like S_R_P, or an entity index into a large domain). The required *answer* is a knowledge object gated by that output: a valid English word from the bank (verified against the wordlist), the pattern-completing word, the fact about the indexed entity. **Planting:** generator picks the word/fact first, chooses a final state encoding it, and inverts the computation (run the CA backward / choose the seed) — or simpler, runs forward and selects instances whose outputs gate at least one valid answer. **What each condition can do:** C0 fails at the computation (calibrated in Experiment 1 — we know exactly where); C1 computes and can even *print the letter bank*, but the scored answer is the word, and the program can't produce wordness — the model would have to embed the lexicon (the gated domain is chosen lexicon-sized precisely so front-loading fails: the model can't know *which* slice it will need, the maximally adaptive case); C∞ computes the bank in call 1, thinks of a word, verifies in call 2. **Predicted:** C0 ≈ chance, C1 ≈ chance + embedded-lexicon overlap, C∞ ≈ probe accuracy — the superadditive signature (H11) in its purest form, with both failure modes independently pre-calibrated (the C0 arm from Experiment 1, the knowledge arm from the probe). **Knobs:** computation depth k (gates the C0/C1 mental-simulation escape), gated-domain size (encoding burden), answers-per-instance (solution density). **Honesty note:** the conjunction is sequential rather than intertwined, so report it like mechanism (f) — an engineered, provable-ceiling sub-experiment, not a discovered gap. Buildability is maximal: it is literally a composition of two things we've already built (Experiment-1 generators + wordlist verifier).

### Hybrid H2 — Inverse TSP art — rank 2: the flagship demo, with real-literature roots

**Construction.** Pick silhouette s from a pre-screened set (~30–50 animals/objects/letters, each stored as a binary mask). Weighted-Voronoi-stipple n points inside the silhouette (Secord's algorithm; standard TSP-art pipeline), optionally add background noise points at lower density. Compute a reference tour at generation time (OR-Tools/LK; exact for small n). **Task:** (a) output a tour of length ≤ (1+ε) × reference; (b) name what the points depict (free-form, scored against a synonym set; 8-way multiple choice as the easy variant). **Verification:** tour length mechanically; label vs. planted s; optional third metric, *drawing fidelity* — fraction of tour-edge length inside the dilated silhouette mask — a fully mechanical "does the drawn path look like the thing" score, since a good tour of stippled points traces the shape (this is the TSP-art observation run in reverse). **What each condition can do:** tour component replays Experiment-1 TSP (C1 commits to a heuristic, C∞ iterates — the known contingent gap); recognition component: C0 must perceive shape from a raw coordinate list (working-memory-bound, and per ASCIIEval-type findings, weak); C1 can write rendering code but never sees the raster — predicted ≈ C0; C∞ renders an ASCII/coordinate raster in call 1 and recognizes at near-probe accuracy. So the two components make *opposite* predictions about where C1 lands — C1 ≈ C∞ on tour, C1 ≈ C0 on recognition — and the composite lands in between, which is exactly A3's "C1 closer to C0" once the recognition component dominates the score. **Knobs:** n (more points = easier recognition, harder TSP — a beautiful tension knob), noise-point fraction (recognition difficulty at fixed tour difficulty), ε, |S| and silhouette confusability, free-form vs. multiple choice. **Risks:** (1) C1 could embed silhouette templates and classify mechanically — for letters (26 simple glyphs) this is feasible and *should* be left as a measurable escape (logging it = planning-to-compute again); for animal silhouettes, writing templates from memory in code is precisely the laborious serialization A3 posits — the attempt incidence is the data; (2) recognition-from-raster must be probe-calibrated per model or the family measures ASCII perception, not the A3 property. The figure (point cloud → model's tour → visible giraffe) sells the paper.

### Hybrid H3 — Planted-glyph maze — rank 3: most controllable geometry, letter-recognition is the easiest percept

**Construction.** Take a letter/digit glyph as a polyline on an m×m grid; thicken to a self-avoiding path from entrance to exit; carve a maze around it such that this is the *unique* solution path (grow a spanning tree of walls off the path; verify uniqueness by exhaustive search); optionally add long decoy corridors that *almost* draw other letters (confusability knob). **Task:** output the solution path AND the character it draws. **Verification:** path validity/uniqueness mechanically; character = planted label. **What each condition can do:** C0 must solve the maze mentally *and* hold the path's 2D shape — double depth-load; C1 writes BFS, gets the path right, but naming the glyph requires perceiving the path it never sees (predicted ≈ chance over the label pool, modulo template-matching code — for 26 alphanumeric glyphs, embedding rough templates is borderline feasible, so treat letters as the calibration rung and arbitrary icon glyphs as the test rung, mirroring H2's risk note); C∞ solves, renders the path as ASCII art on the grid, reads the letter. **Knobs:** maze size (formal difficulty), glyph resolution (recognition difficulty), label-pool size, decoy-glyph confusability, multi-glyph paths ("the path spells CAT") for compositional scaling. **Why rank 3 and not higher:** it shares H2's mechanism but with less demo value and a real template-embedding escape at small label pools; its advantage is total geometric control and the cheapest recognition probe (grid-rendered letters are the most recognizable text-percepts per the ASCII-recognition literature). **Variants under the same schema (mention, don't build first):** SAT instances whose unique satisfying assignment bitmaps a glyph on a grid (planting: take the bitmap as the assignment, generate clauses it satisfies, add constraints until the count is 1 via a model counter — heavier generation, same logic); sorting/permutation tasks whose output order spells a hidden *clue* whose answer is then required (this degenerates to H1 and is better built as H1).

**Ranked hybrids: H1 (build first — one week, provable C1 ceiling, purest superadditivity), H2 (build second — the flagship figure and the real A3 story with both components), H3 (build if H2's recognition calibration proves noisy — it's the controllable fallback).**

## Part 3 — Literature delta (2024–2026), tight

What follows deliberately excludes everything already in C1_CINF_GAP.md and VARIATION_DIMENSIONS_LIT.md (SweepClip, CrossWordBench, LR2Bench, BALROG, OQA/BED-LLM, FunSearch/Eureka, furniture-layout line).

**Constrained text generation: conjunction failure is established; no code conditions anywhere.** COLLIE ([Yao et al. 2023](https://arxiv.org/abs/2307.08689)) built grammar-based compositional constraints and found fixed constraint types already easy for GPT-4; its successors sharpened the conjunction finding: ULTRABENCH ([Findings of EMNLP 2025](https://aclanthology.org/2025.findings-emnlp.835/)) packs ~70 mixed hard/soft constraints per instance and finds models satisfy individual constraints at >70% but **consistently fail to satisfy all simultaneously**, with structural constraints hardest — that is the A3 property measured at the text level (units fine, conjunction fails), but with no knowledge probe, no condition ladder, and LLM-judged soft constraints. ComplexBench ([Wen et al., NeurIPS 2024](https://keg.cs.tsinghua.edu.cn/jietang/publications/NeurIPS24-Wen-et-al-Complex-Bench.pdf)) taxonomizes constraint *composition* types (And/Chain/Selection); SEQUOR ([2026](https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.06353)) moves it multi-turn. None runs a write-a-program condition — reasonable for prose tasks, but it means **nobody has asked whether code rescues constrained generation**, which is exactly our C1 column.

**The closest existing thing to a direct-query-vs-in-search measurement: KITAB.** KITAB ([Abdin et al., ICLR 2024](https://proceedings.iclr.cc/paper_files/paper/2024/hash/82846e19e6d42ebfd4ace4361def29ae-Abstract-Conference.html)) tests constraint-satisfaction retrieval ("list books by author X whose titles contain no proper noun") against a verified book database and decouples failure modes by entity popularity and constraint type — models know the books (popularity-gated) yet fail constrained listing, and giving full context "is not helpful for satisfying constraints, identifying fundamental barriers." This is the A3 dissociation in the wild, but with no formal search component, no code conditions, and no parametric generation. Our families are KITAB-with-a-search-core-and-a-condition-ladder.

**Word/puzzle game benchmarks exist; none has a one-shot-program condition.** TextGames ([2025](https://arxiv.org/abs/2502.18431)) includes Anagram Scribble and Crossword Arranger with single-turn vs. multi-turn-feedback evaluation — models improve with feedback but the comparison is direct-answer vs. direct-answer-with-retries, never write-code-once; Word Game Bench (Wordle/Connections daily puzzles, contamination-resistant by construction) likewise. The C1 column on lexical-search games appears to be unmeasured anywhere — treat as a positioning asset, same as C1_CINF_GAP.md §2.1 concluded for formal problems.

**Knowing-the-parts-vs-composing literature (weights-level analog of A3).** The "compositionality gap" (Press et al. 2023, [arXiv 2210.03350](https://arxiv.org/abs/2210.03350)): ~40% failure on 2-hop questions whose both hops the model answers correctly in isolation — single-hop competence does not compose, the question-answering shadow of A3. Mechanistic follow-ups: *How Do Language Models Compose Functions?* (2025, **[arXiv ID unverified — found via aggregator]**) confirms the gap persists and identifies idiomatic (direct x→g(f(x))) vs. compositional internal mechanisms; the Coverage Principle ([2025](https://arxiv.org/abs/2505.20278)) gives a data-coverage framework predicting exactly when pattern-matching composition fails; CREME ([Findings of ACL 2024](https://aclanthology.org/2024.findings-acl.576.pdf)) locates the failure in mid-layer attention. Useful framing for the paper: A3 families put the *same* dissociation under a condition ladder where the composition can be outsourced to a machine — the gap mechanisms literature says the deficit is real and architectural, we ask which execution topology repairs it.

**Creative generation under formal constraints: form is nearly solved, the lexicon-in-the-loop is the binding resource.** POEMetric ([ICLR 2026](https://arxiv.org/abs/2604.03695)): top models hit high form accuracy (meter/rhyme ~4.3/5) while losing badly to humans on judged quality — formal compliance is no longer the hard part *for standard English forms*, supporting our choice to push the formal side harder (chains, conjunctions, frequency floors) rather than rely on sonnets being difficult. Agirrezabal & co. ([ICCC 2025](https://computationalcreativity.net/iccc25/papers/iccc25-agirrezabal2025refining.pdf)): iterative feedback fixes syllable counts but *breaks rhyme in the process* (constraint interference under iteration — a C∞ failure mode worth pre-registering for F5). Most striking for mechanism (e): the Tang-poetry system Xunzi-Yayun-R1 ([npj Heritage Science 2025](https://www.nature.com/articles/s40494-025-02087-x)) lifts rhyming accuracy from 65.8 to 87.9 *by RAG against an external rhyme table* — direct evidence that putting the lexicon inside the loop, rather than asking the weights to serialize it, is what moves constrained generation; our F5 measures the same resource via the condition ladder instead of RAG.

**TSP art / mathematical art: a mature practice, never used for evaluation.** Bosch & Herman, *Continuous Line Drawings via the Traveling Salesman Problem* (Oper. Res. Letters 2004; [PDF](https://www2.oberlin.edu/math/faculty/bosch/cld.pdf)) established stipple-then-solve; Kaplan & Bosch's TSP Art ([survey page](https://cs.uwaterloo.ca/~csk/other/tsp/)) refined it with weighted Voronoi stippling (Secord 2002) + Concorde; Bosch's *Opt Art* (Princeton UP 2019) is the book-length treatment. The entire literature runs generation-direction (image → points → tour-as-rendering); we found **no use of the inverse direction (points → hidden image) as a test of any solver, human or machine** — H2's construction appears genuinely novel, and the pipeline components are all off-the-shelf.

**Text-rendered shape perception: weak, measurable, and the calibration constraint for our hybrids.** ArtPrompt/ViTC ([Jiang et al. 2024](https://arxiv.org/abs/2402.11753) **[ID unverified]**): GPT-4 at 25.2% on single-character ASCII-art recognition; ASCIIEval ([2024, v2 2025](https://arxiv.org/abs/2410.01733)): 3K human-drawn ASCII artworks, proprietary models exceed 70% only on favorable categories, accuracy sensitive to art length; ASCIIArt-Bench ([2026](https://arxiv.org/html/2603.14505v1)) adds generation+understanding jointly. Two implications: (1) H2/H3's recognition probes must be per-model gates, not assumptions; (2) the C∞-renders-then-perceives loop is itself a capability with a measurable ceiling — our hybrids effectively put ASCIIEval *inside* a search task, which nobody has done.

**What's missing (the holes we'd fill):** (1) no benchmark measures the same knowledge units direct-query vs. inside-search with any condition ladder (KITAB is closest, no ladder, no search core); (2) no constrained-generation or word-game benchmark runs a one-shot-program condition, so the rescue fraction ρ is unmeasured everywhere; (3) no planted-semantic-ground-truth construction exists in print — judged scoring is the universal default for "looks like / sounds like / is meaningful" side conditions (ULTRABENCH's soft constraints, POEMetric's judges), and the planting trick removes it; (4) the front-loadable vs. adaptive knowledge-demand distinction (criterion ii′) appears unarticulated, as does its sharp prediction (matched-lexicon families with opposite ρ).

## Summary of recommendations

Build order: **F2+F6 matched pair → H1 trapdoor → F1 crosswords (planned) → H2 inverse TSP art → F3 table-size sweep.** Pre-register: ρ < 0.5 on F1/F2/F4/F5 at probe ≥ 90%; ρ > 0.7 on F6 and small-table F3; C1 hardcoded-knowledge incidence rises as table size falls; H1 shows C∞ > C1 ≈ C0 ≈ chance with a derivable C1 ceiling; H2's components dissociate (tour: C1 ≈ C∞; recognition: C1 ≈ C0). Each is specific enough to be wrong, which is the house style.
